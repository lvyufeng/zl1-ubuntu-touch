   # decoder: latency-us is a big-endian u32, so 0x64 = 100 us (system-wfi), 0x15e = 350 us (system-ret),
   # 0x2af8 = 11000 us (system-fpc = FULL POWER COLLAPSE of the whole SoC), and the per-CPU ones are
   # 20 / 40 / 80 us. The deeper the state, the larger the exit latency -- which is exactly why a device
   # that may not use them stays warm while idle.
   #
   # NOTE the THREE shapes, because it cost the probe a defect: the whole-SoC levels are at depth 2
   # under the lpm node, the L2 ones at depth 3, and the per-CPU ones at depth 4 under a `qcom,pm-cpu`
   # node. A fixed-depth glob silently loses the whole-SoC ones -- the ones that matter.

==============================================================================
5. the two parsers (both read-only, both open the file O_RDONLY)
==============================================================================
fdtwalk.py -- walks an FDT blob and prints "path  prop = value" for nodes matching an optional argument:
  header is 10 big-endian u32 at offset 0: magic, totalsize, off_struct, off_strings, off_rsvmap, ...
  then a token stream: 1=BEGIN_NODE (NUL-terminated name, 4-byte aligned), 2=END_NODE, 3=PROP
  (u32 len, u32 nameoff into the strings block, then len bytes padded to 4), 4=NOP, 9=END.
  `size_struct` is at offset 36 and NOT the same field as `size_strings` at offset 32 -- mixing those
  two up makes the walk terminate immediately and print nothing, which is how the first version of the
  node dumper "found no match" in a tree full of matches.

TWO RENDERING TRAPS in the value printer, both hit here (the raw hex is in the lines above):
  * a u32 value of 0x00000064 is four bytes of which three are NUL, so a naive "is it printable text?"
    test says YES and you get `"   d"` instead of 100. That is why section 4's numbers are shown with
    their hex in a comment.
  * a device-tree string property is NUL-terminated and can hold SEVERAL strings separated by NUL, so
    splitting on NUL and printing the pieces is the honest rendering.
